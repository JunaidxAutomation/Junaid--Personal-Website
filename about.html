How I Think

Architecture decisions are made for production from day one.

This is the credibility marker that actually matters for a technical buyer: not what I say about my work, but how I make decisions when no one's watching the intermediate steps. Three principles govern every build.

01

No MVP that needs a rewrite in six months

Row-level security, proper data isolation, and test coverage go in from the first commit — not bolted on once something "works." A prototype that can't become the real thing wastes everyone's time twice.

02

Systems thinking before syntax

Three years of legal study trains a specific habit: find the edge case that breaks the general rule before you commit to the rule. That's the same instinct that catches a race condition or a missing auth check before it ships.

03

Say no to scope I can't deliver well

I structure client work around a fixed weekly capacity rather than accepting more than I can deliver properly. If a project needs more bandwidth than I have that month, you hear that upfront — not after a missed deadline.
